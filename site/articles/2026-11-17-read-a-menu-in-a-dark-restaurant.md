Menus are the classic small-print crime scene: tiny type, low light, and a waiter standing there.

## 1. Light it, but at an angle

Pointing the flashlight straight at a glossy menu bounces light into the lens and washes out the words. Tilt the phone a little, or hold the light off to one side. You want soft, even light on the page, not a spotlight.

## 2. Zoom with your fingers, not your face

Spread two fingers to zoom. If the text goes soft, back the phone up a little and zoom less. Close and sharp beats far and zoomed.

## 3. Steady your hands

Rest your elbows on the table. Both hands on the phone. Hold your breath for a second as you read. Dim light makes the camera use slower exposure, which turns shaky hands into blur.

## 4. Freeze it

Some cameras and magnifier apps let you freeze the picture. Then you can read the whole line without holding your arm out. [See It Yo](../../) does this with one tap: it opens already zoomed, turns the light on by itself when the room is dark, and freezes the frame when you tap the screen.

## Quick checklist

- Light angled, not straight on
- Zoom a little, not a lot
- Elbows on the table
- Freeze, read, tap to go live again
