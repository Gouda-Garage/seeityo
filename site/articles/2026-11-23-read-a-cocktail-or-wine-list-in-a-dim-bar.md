<div class="answer">Open your phone camera, zoom in on the list, tilt the light so it doesn't glare, and freeze the picture so you can read the whole thing without holding your arm out.</div>

Bar lists are made for mood, not for reading. The room is dark, the paper is dark, and the type is tiny. Here is how to beat that.

## Use the light, but keep it low

If your phone has a flashlight, point it at the list from the side. Straight-on light bounces off glossy paper and hides the words. Side light shows the print clearly and bothers nobody at the next stool.

## Zoom a little, then hold still

Spread two fingers to zoom. If the words turn soft, back the phone away a bit and zoom less. Rest your elbows on the bar and use both hands. In low light the camera takes a slower picture, so a shaky hand turns into blur.

## Freeze it

A frozen picture lets you read every drink and price, then look up and talk. [See It Yo](../../) opens already zoomed, turns the light on when the room is dark, and freezes the frame when you tap. It saves no photos, so your night out stays yours.

## Read the small stuff last

Look at the drink name first, then the short description, then the price. Ask the bartender about anything you can't make out. They answer that question all day.

If you also struggle with restaurant menus, see [how to read a menu in a dark restaurant](../read-a-menu-in-a-dark-restaurant/).

## Quick checklist

- Light from the side, not straight on
- Zoom a little, not a lot
- Elbows on the bar, two hands
- Tap to freeze, read, tap to go live again
