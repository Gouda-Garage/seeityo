<div class="answer">A flashlight can make text worse because it causes glare on shiny surfaces and washes out the page. Tilt the phone or move the light to the side, and turn it off if the room is already bright enough.</div>

More light should mean easier reading. But anyone who has pointed a phone light at a glossy label knows it can go the other way.

## Glare is the main problem

Shiny paper, plastic wrap and laminated menus act like small mirrors. A flashlight pointed straight at them sends a bright spot right back into the lens. The spot covers the words, and the camera darkens everything else to cope.

## The fix: light from the side

Tilt the phone a little, or hold the light off to one side. The page still gets lit, but the bounce goes somewhere else. Even a small change can make the words appear.

## Too much light up close

Very close to the page, a flashlight can be harsh and uneven. You get a bright center and dark edges. Move the phone back a bit and zoom in instead of crowding the print.

## Sometimes no flashlight is better

If the room already has soft light, the flashlight adds glare for no gain. Try both ways and keep the one that shows more words.

## Quick checklist

- Tilt the phone to dodge glare
- Move back and zoom in
- Try with and without the light
- Freeze the clearest frame
