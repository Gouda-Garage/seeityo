<div class="answer">For most people, close-up focusing gets harder somewhere in the 40s. It's very common and usually gradual. An eye exam is the real answer. A phone magnifier is a handy bridge.</div>

One day the menu is fine. A few months later you're holding it at arm's length. You're not alone.

## What's going on

The lens inside your eye gets stiffer with age, so it's slower to change focus between far and near. Eye doctors call this **presbyopia**. It usually becomes noticeable in the 40s and tends to keep changing for a while.

## Signs people notice

- Holding things farther away to read them
- Needing brighter light than before
- Eyes feeling tired after close work
- Small print in dim rooms being the first thing to go

## What to do

1. **Get an eye exam.** It can sort out normal age change from something else, and it's the way to get the right prescription.
2. **Keep a pair of readers where you need them**, like the car, your bag and the kitchen.
3. **Use your phone as a backup.** A camera or a magnifier app like [See It Yo](../../) can save you when the glasses are somewhere else.
