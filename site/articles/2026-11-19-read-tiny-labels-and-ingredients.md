<div class="answer">For food and household labels, a phone camera with good light and a steady hand works well. For medicine, use the phone as a helper, not the final word. If you're not sure, ask a pharmacist.</div>

Ingredient lists and warning text are set in type that seems to be designed to be skipped.

## Reading a food or household label

1. Get bright, even light. Side light beats straight-on light.
2. Hold the item still, or rest it on a table.
3. Zoom the camera until the words are clear. Tap the print so it focuses.
4. Read one line at a time. If it blurs, move closer and zoom less.

## Curved bottles and shiny labels

Glare is the enemy. Turn the bottle slowly while you look at the screen until the shine slides off the words.

## Medicine is different

A phone screen can mislead you: a blurry 8 can look like a 3. For dosage and warnings:

- Check the number more than once, in good light.
- Ask a pharmacist to read it or explain it.
- Ask the pharmacy for a **large-print label**. Many offer it.
- Never guess.
