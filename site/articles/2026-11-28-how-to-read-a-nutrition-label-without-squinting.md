Nutrition labels are a lot of numbers in a small box, often printed on a curved package. That is a hard thing to read.

## Zoom in on the box

Hold the phone a little way from the package and zoom until the label fills the screen. Curved cans and bags are easier if you flatten the package a bit or turn it so the label faces the camera straight on.

## Read in this order

1. **Serving size.** Every number below it is for one serving. A package can hold more than one.
2. **Calories.** Shown in large type, per serving.
3. **The nutrients you watch.** Look at the lines that matter to you.
4. **%Daily Value.** A rough guide: 5% or less per serving is low, and 20% or more is high.

## Cut the glare

Shiny packaging bounces light. Tilt the phone, or move so the store light isn't reflecting into the lens.

## Not a medical tool

[See It Yo](../../) only magnifies. It doesn't judge food or give health advice. If you follow a special diet or have an allergy, ask your doctor, dietitian or pharmacist, and read the label carefully yourself.

The app opens already zoomed and freezes the frame on a tap, so you can read the whole label in a store aisle. For ingredients, see [how to read tiny labels and ingredients](../read-tiny-labels-and-ingredients/).

## Quick checklist

- Serving size first
- Flatten or turn curved packages
- Tilt away from glare
- Freeze and read
