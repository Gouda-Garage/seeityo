The big number on a shelf tag is the price. The useful number is the small one next to it.

## What the unit price tells you

A unit price is the cost per standard amount, such as per ounce or per count. It lets you compare a big box with a small one without doing math in the aisle.

## Find it and zoom in

On many tags the unit price sits in small type near the edge. Low shelves are the worst: you bend down, the tag is in shadow, and the print is tiny. Hold the phone a bit back from the tag and zoom until the small numbers fill the screen. Freeze the picture and read it while standing up straight.

## Check the unit before you compare

One tag may say per ounce and the next say per pound or per count. Make sure both use the same unit. If they don't, ask a store worker or do a quick calculation.

## Watch for old tags

Tags can be out of date or on the wrong spot on the shelf. If the price at the register is different, ask the store about it.

- Find the small unit price
- Zoom and freeze
- Same unit on both tags
- Ask about tags that look wrong
