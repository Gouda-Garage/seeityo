<div class="answer">Open your camera, point it at the print, spread two fingers to zoom, and tap the screen to focus. That's it. Your phone is already a magnifying glass.</div>

If you don't carry reader glasses, this is the trick that saves you at the pharmacy, the restaurant and the hardware store.

## The 4-step version

1. **Open the camera app.** Don't take a photo. Just use the live view.
2. **Spread two fingers** on the screen to zoom in until the words are big enough.
3. **Tap the print** so the camera focuses on it.
4. **Hold still.** Rest your elbows on the table or your body. Shaky hands blur zoomed text fast.

## Why it sometimes looks blurry

Zooming too far makes the picture soft. If the text looks mushy, move the phone closer to the page and zoom a little less. Light matters more than people think: a bright page gives you a sharper picture.

## Make it faster

The slow part is the fiddling: open the app, find the zoom, tap to focus, find the flashlight. If you do this a lot, a one-tap magnifier app saves you those steps. [See It Yo](../../) is a free Android beta built for exactly that. It opens already zoomed in and turns the light on by itself in the dark. It saves nothing.

## When to skip the phone

If it's a medicine label or a safety warning, don't trust a blurry screen. Ask a pharmacist or a person. See our article on [reading medicine and ingredient labels](../read-tiny-labels-and-ingredients/).
