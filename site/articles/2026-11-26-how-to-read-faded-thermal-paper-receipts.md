Many store receipts are printed on heat-sensitive paper. There is no ink. The printer heats the paper and it turns dark where it's heated. That makes receipts cheap to print, and also easy to fade.

## Why they go pale

Heat and light can both darken or fade the paper over time. A receipt left on a car dashboard or in a sunny window can go pale quickly. Even a pocket or wallet can wear the print.

## Reading one that has faded

Faded print is usually still there, just light gray. Try this:

- Use soft, even light. Avoid hard glare.
- Tilt the page a little. Some angles make the gray stand out.
- Zoom in on one line at a time, not the whole page.
- Hold the phone steady with both hands.

## Slowing the fade

If you need a receipt for a return or warranty, check it soon after you get it. Keep it away from heat and sun. If you want a record, take a clear picture of it with your regular camera while it's still dark.

## Quick checklist

- Soft light, no glare
- Tilt to find the angle
- Zoom one line at a time
- Store receipts away from heat and sun
